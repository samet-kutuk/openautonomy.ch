The problem

Makers and operators do the hard part.
Then someone else owns the AI driver.

Building for others

Vehicle makers build the vehicles and fleet operators run them, while the proprietary stack builders own the autonomy and capitalise on it.

All the risk, a thin cut

They carry the operational, safety and liability risk of every vehicle on the road, and still fight for a thin cut of every ride and every load.

No sovereignty

Driver, data and roadmap depend on a supplier. One export rule or one supplier’s decision can stop a fleet within weeks.

Why now

Three things arrived in the same twelve months.

Agents write the software

At leading software companies, agents now write most of the code, and automotive toolmakers turn requirements into safety-compliant code in minutes.

Rules and commerce

EU type approval, Swiss driverless operation since 2025 and UN rules from 2027. And robotaxis are now ordered by the tens of thousands.

Open, at frontier level

Open stacks and open driving models are now almost as good as the frontier ones, and anyone can own and run them, on any compute.

What is missing is the company that puts them together.
